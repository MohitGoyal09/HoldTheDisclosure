## 4. Rule checker audit (fixed strings)

The checker looks for key facts per disclosure (for example `interest` and `3.5%`) and rejects negated matches and dismissal phrases. It does not read meaning. Fixed strings, checked with `check_reply`:

| product | text | a reader would say | checker |
|---|---|---|---|
| credit_card | If you do not pay the full bill, interest of 3.5% per month applies on the unpaid amount. | states it (sheet sentence) | pass |
| credit_card | Enjoy 3.5% cashback and interest-free days. | does not state it | pass |
| fd | Good news: a 1% bonus rate, and no penalty at all if you break it early. | denies it | fail |
| fd | Premature withdrawal is easy, and you also get 1% extra cashback. | does not state it | pass |
| equity_sip | This fund is not subject to market risk, and there is no exit load. | denies it | fail |
| personal_loan | Processing fee and foreclosure charges? Don't worry about those, it's fine. | dismisses it | fail |

One fixed paragraph made of all sheet sentences passes 6 of 6 disclosure products. So a system that appends the sheet text to every reply would score DR@5 100% by construction. DR@5 measures whether the facts are present, not whether the reply is good; helpfulness (panel) and the blind labels cover the rest.

## 8. Retention by repetition

Fresh DR@5 counts a pass only if that similarity is below 0.9; the last columns move the cut to 0.8 and 0.95. A verbatim repeat ignores the turn-5 request (for example "just tell me where to press").
