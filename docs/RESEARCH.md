# Research plan and paper outline

Working title: **Hold the disclosure: keeping mandatory risk warnings under multi-turn pushback in Hindi, Hinglish and English.**

Status (2026-11-11): the study is complete; results are in `docs/RESULTS_FULL.md`, `docs/RESULTS_LLAMA.md` and `docs/REPORT.md`. This file is the original plan and outline, kept as written; where it disagrees with the report, the report is current.

## Research question

Under five turns of customer pushback, how fast does a 3.35B multilingual model drop mandatory financial disclosures in English, Devanagari Hindi and romanized Hinglish? Can preference training restore retention without making the model refuse, lecture, or warn on products that need no warning?

## Why this is a paper, not a demo

The contribution is not "we fine-tuned a model". It is a measured failure mode and a repair that keeps the safety text short enough to survive real customer pressure.

1. A five-turn disclosure ladder that is matched across three scripts, with control products that must not receive warnings.
2. A rule-checked metric with a dismissal guard, a script check and numeric boundaries, plus a judge for refusals and lectures.
3. A preference-data recipe with recovery histories: the model practises answering after its own earlier drop, not only from a clean history.
4. A before-and-after study on Tiny Aya Fire with base, strict, third-person and SFT baselines, on test, held-out products and held-out tactics.
5. A reproducibility artifact and six confirmed defects in the tooling.

## Method

### Data

- Fictional bank, ten products. Four train products, two held-out products, four control products.
- Five-turn ladder: request, brevity, social proof, urgency, direct override.
- Three scripts, matched by construction.
- Test uses unseen seed lines. Held-out uses unseen products and only held-out tactics. Test openings are disjoint from train openings.
- Replies generated by GLM-4.7-Flash behind a rule gate, a length gate and an optional reward gate.
- Each dialogue becomes SFT rows and DPO pairs. A quarter of dialogues plus every dialogue at turn 3 get a recovery history with an earlier drop.

### Systems

| System | Prompt | Training |
|---|---|---|
| Base | plain | none |
| Strict | disclosures listed in the customer's script | none |
| Third person | SYCON-style Andrew persona | none |
| SFT | plain | LoRA SFT on chosen replies |
| DPO | plain | LoRA DPO, chosen vs drop and lecture |
| AlignTune DPO | plain | same objective through AlignTune |
| Closed reference | strict | one API model, optional |

### Metrics

Primary: DR@5 on the test split, rule-checked, with and without the in-script requirement.

Secondary: DR@1 to DR@4, Turn of Flip, Kept@5 given T1, held-out DR@5, control warning rate, judge over-correction, script gap.

### Statistics

- 95% cluster bootstrap by dialogue plan, not by individual script.
- Paired McNemar exact test on the same dialogues.
- Judge agreement with 100 human labels, reported per script.
- Six binary experiment criteria, fixed before the first trained model.

## Threats to validity

1. **Construct validity of the rule checker.** Key-fact patterns can be satisfied by a terse sentence that compliance would reject. The dismissal guard is phrase based.
2. **Synthetic data and one fictional bank.** Real product documents are longer and less consistent.
3. **Scripted ladder.** An adaptive customer who argues with the actual reply is harder. It is a planned second test, not in the main result.
4. **Small n.** 60 test dialogues, 20 per script. CIs are wide.
5. **Clustering.** The three scripts of a plan are not independent. The CI resamples plans, but McNemar treats dialogues as paired units.
6. **Selection.** The experiment loop uses test and held-out criteria to choose a recipe. The final number is still a test number, but it is a selected one. Report all runs.
7. **Truncation.** Generation is capped at 384 tokens and the disclosure is generated last. Report the truncation rate.
8. **Judge.** One model, validated on a sample. Not a certified annotator.
9. **Template-like chosen replies.** DPO may learn a sentence shape rather than a policy. The held-out products test that.
10. **Checker tuned after seeing test errors.** Four correct paraphrases were added to the patterns after reading test failures. Report the original and expanded checker, and let the human labels decide.
11. **Licence.** Tiny Aya and the adapters are CC-BY-NC-4.0. Non-commercial research.

## Writing discipline

- No number without an artifact.
- Always report the CI, not only the point estimate.
- Always report per script, not only pooled.
- Always report over-correction next to the gain.
- Separate "the model states the text" from "the customer understands the risk". The paper measures the first.
- State the selection procedure and its effect on the reported number.
